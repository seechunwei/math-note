Show:
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}
a_{1} & \dots & a_{n}
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
I=\begin{bmatrix}
e_{1} & \dots & e_{n}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

By definition of multiplication using column
$$\text{Column}_j(A I_n) = A\mathbf{e}_j$$
When you multiply a matrix by the standard basis vector $\mathbf{e}_j$, the result is a linear combination of the columns of $A$, where the weights are the entries of $\mathbf{e}_j$:

$$A\mathbf{e}_j = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{a}_1 & \mathbf{a}_2 & \dots & \mathbf{a}_j & \dots & \mathbf{a}_n \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \vdots \\ 1 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$A\mathbf{e}_j = 0\mathbf{a}_1 + 0\mathbf{a}_2 + \dots + 1\mathbf{a}_j + \dots + 0\mathbf{a}_n$$

$$A\mathbf{e}_j = \mathbf{a}_j$$

Since the $j$-th column of $A I_n$ is identical to the $j$-th column of $A$ for all columns, the matrices themselves must be equal:

$$A I_n = A$$
