> [!theorem]
> Let $A$ and $B$ be square matrices. If $AB=I$, then $A$ and $B$ are both invertible, with $B=A^{-1}$ and $A=B^{-1}$.

^481589

Proof:
Suppose $A$ and $B$ be square matrices. and $AB=I$. 

To show $B$ is invertible, we just need to show that $Bx=0$ has only trivial solution.

$$
\begin{align}
A(Bx)&=A(0) \\
(AB)x&=0 \\
Ix&=0 \\
x&=0
\end{align}
$$


Thus, 
$$
\begin{align}
AB&=I \\
ABB^{-1}&=IB^{-1} \\
A&=B^{-1}
\end{align}
$$
Since $B^{-1}$ is invertible, it follow that $A$ is also invertible.

### Invertible Linear Transformation

Inverse matrix can be view as inverse function. 

A linear transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to \mathbb{R}^{n}$ is said to be invertible if there exists a function $S:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to \mathbb{R}^{n}$ such that

$$
T(S(x))=x
$$
for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ (x comes from codomain) and

$$
S(T(x))=x
$$
for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ (x comes from domain).

==Remark==
Notice that the dimension of domain and codomain must be same for inverse to exist. (size of standard matrix $A$)

==Theorem 9==
Let $T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to \mathbb{R}^{n}$ be a linear transformation and let $A$ be the standard matrix for $T$. Then $T$ is invertible iff $A$ is an invertible matrix.

In that case, the linear transformation $S$ given by $S(x)=A^{-1}x$ is the unique function satisfying equations (1) and (2).


Proof:
$T$ is invertible iff 
there exists a transformation $S: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ such that:
1. $S(T(x)) = x$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$
2. $T(S(x)) = x$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$

Let $B$ be the $n\times n$ standard matrix of $S$. Thus, (composition of linear transformation can be viewed as the product of their standard matrix)

$$
\begin{align}
S(T(x))&=x \\
B(Ax)&=x \\
(BA)x&=x
\end{align}
$$


Since $S(T(x)) = x$ for all $x$, it must be that $(BA)x = Ix$. This implies that:

$$BA = I$$
Thus, for square matrix if $BA=I$, then $A$ is invertible and $B=A^{-1}$.(By fact above)

==Remark==
Why  $AC=BC$ does not imply $A=B$? it is because $AC=BC$ only implies they are the same if $x$ comes from the column of $C$. But $T$ is true for all $x$.

(Continue) Suppose conversely $A$ is an $n\times n$ invertible matrix. Thus, let $S(x)=A^{-1}x$. Thus,

$$
\begin{align}
S(T(x))&=A^{-1}(Ax)=(A^{-1}A)x=Ix=x \\
T(S(x))&= A(A^{-1}x)=(AA^{-1})x=Ix=x
\end{align}
$$

Thus, $S$ is the inverse of $T$. Q.E.D

==Theorem==
Suppose that $T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to \mathbb{R}^{n}$ is a linear transformation. Then $T$ is invertible iff $T$ is one-to-one and onto.

Suppose $T$ is invertible, by theorem above the standard matrix $A$ is an invertible matrix. Thus, by Invertible Matrix Theorem the linear transformation $T$ is one-to-one and onto.

Conversely (also use invertible matrix theorem)
