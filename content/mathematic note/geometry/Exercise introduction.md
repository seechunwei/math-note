13) is the sum (difference) of given segments unique? Give an ex-ample of two distinct segments which both are sums of the given segments. Show that these distinct segments are congruent.

**The Answer:** No, the segment itself is **not** unique, but its _length_ (congruence class) is.

**The Trap:** In geometry, a "segment" is a specific physical collection of points in space. If I draw a 5-inch line on my desk, and you draw a 5-inch line on your notebook, we both have segments of the exact same length, but they are **distinct segments** because they are made of completely different points in different locations.

**The Example:** Let's say we are adding segment _a_ and segment _b_.

- On the left side of your paper, pick a starting Point A. Add segment _a_ to get AB, and then add segment _b_ to get BC. Your sum is the new segment **AC**.
    
- On the right side of your paper, pick a starting Point X. Add segment _a_ to get XY, and then add segment _b_ to get YZ. Your sum is the new segment **XZ**.
    

Segment AC and Segment XZ are **two distinct segments** (they exist in different places on the paper), but they are both the sum of _a_ + _b_.

**The Proof (Showing they are congruent):** You can prove these distinct segments are congruent using the exact same logic you used in your previous textbook answer!

 By definition, segment AC is made of parts _a_ and _b_, and segment XZ is also made of parts _a_ and _b_.
 
Because part _a_ is congruent to part _a_, and part _b_ is congruent to part _b_, there exists an isometry (a perfect movement) that maps the starting Point A exactly onto Point X.

 Because isometries preserve straight lines and distances, the rest of segment AC will perfectly overlap segment XZ.
 
 Since they superimpose on each other perfectly, segment AC is congruent to segment XZ.
