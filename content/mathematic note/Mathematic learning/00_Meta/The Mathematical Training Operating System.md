🧠 The Mathematical Training Operating System (MT-OS)

This workflow is divided into three distinct cycles: the Training Cycle (Daily), the Alchemy Cycle (Weekly/Project), and the Architecture Cycle (Monthly).

1. The Training Cycle: The Daily Engine

Goal: Convert raw struggle into "Specific Questions" and "First-Pass Solutions."

Action: Create a "Session Note" (e.g., 2026-07-10 Training)

1. Isolate (The Problem): Select a problem. Define its boundary.
  - Obsidian Tip: Use a #status/attempting tag.
2. The Staged Struggle (The Attempt):
  - Set a timer for 15 minutes of "Zero New Questions."
  - Do not look at the solution.
  - Document the "breaking point." Literally write: "I am stuck at [Step X] because I don't know how to [Specific Operation]."
3. Granular Inquiry (The Specific Question):
  - Convert your struggle into a prompt for AI or a search query.
  - Example: Instead of "How to solve this integral?", use "How to handle a non-elementary $e^{y^2}$ term in a double integral inner loop?"
4. Targeted Acquisition (Feedback):
  - Use AI/Textbooks to bridge only that specific gap.
  - Write the solution in your own words (Self-Explanation).
5. Anti-Overfitting Check (Verification):
  - Transfer Test: Immediately ask AI or search for a "spiritually resonant but formally different" problem. Solve it.

---
2. The Alchemy Cycle: Turning Solutions into Tools

Goal: Move from a solved problem to a "Zero-Load" mental shortcut.

Once a problem is solved and verified, it is "raw data." You must now Encapsulate it.

Action: Create/Update a "Tool Note" in your Toolbox folder.

Follow your Abstraction Trilogy:
1. 🏷️ Name it (The Index): Give it a sharp, actionable name.
  - Example: "Bound the integral by something simpler."
2. 📦 Encapsulate (The Core Concept):
  - Input: When do I trigger this? (Conditions/Patterns).
  - Logic: What is the essence? (The "Gold").
  - Output: What does it produce?
  - Boundary: Where does this tool fail? (The "Expert" layer).
3. 💎 Crystallize (The 3 Examples):
  - Obsidian Tip: Use [[Example A]], [[Example B]], [[Example C]].

---
3. The Architecture Cycle: The Mastery Map

Goal: Ensure you aren't an "Information Hoarder" but a "Strategy Encapsulator."

Action: The "Field Review" (Monthly)

Review a specific domain (e.g., Linear Algebra) and answer these Mastery Questions:
- [ ] What are the 5 primary problems this field solves?
- [ ] Which "Mental Shortcuts" (Tools) are my primary weapons for these problems?
- [ ] Which tools have the most "fragile" boundaries (where I often make mistakes)?
- [ ] Can I explain the "Motivation" behind the key definitions without looking at my notes?

The Role of AI in this Workflow

Instead of using AI to "get the answer," use it as a Cognitive Mirror:
1. The Gap Finder: "I am stuck at [X]. Don't give me the answer; give me a hint about the underlying property I'm missing."
2. The Abstraction Partner: "Here is a solution I found. Help me strip the specific numbers and identify the 'Core Logic' for encapsulation."
3. The Adversary: "I've encapsulated this tool. Give me a problem that looks like it fits this tool but actually falls outside its boundary to test my understanding."
