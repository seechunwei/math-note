Imagine a right angle triangle where $a,b$ are acute angle. Thus, $a+b=90^\circ$. $b=a-90^\circ$.


$$
\begin{align}
\sin a&= \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}= \frac{\text{Adjacent to }(90^\circ -a)}{\text{Hypotenuse}} \\
&=\cos (90^\circ -a) \\
&=\cos b
\end{align}
$$

It is the same as other trigonometric function.

These identities used for simplifying trigonometric expressions.

### How to prove an identity?

Consider the equation below

$$
\sin x=-\sin x
$$
We square both sides, we get the equation
$$
\sin ^{2}x=\sin ^{2}x
$$
Does this mean that the original equation is an identity? No because the operation is not reversible.

To prove a trigonometric identity
1) Start with one side
2) Use known identities
3) Convert Sines and Cosines
