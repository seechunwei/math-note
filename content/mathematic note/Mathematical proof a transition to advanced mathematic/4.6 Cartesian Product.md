### **Theorem:**

For any sets $A, B, C,$ and $D$:

$$(A \times B) \cap (C \times D) = (A \cap C) \times (B \cap D)$$

### **Proof:**

**Part 1: $(\subseteq)$ Forward Inclusion**

We simplify the Left Hand Side (LHS) to show it is contained in the Right Hand Side (RHS).

Let $z$ be an arbitrary element of $(A \times B) \cap (C \times D)$.

By the definition of intersection, $z \in (A \times B)$ and $z \in (C \times D)$.

Since $z$ is in a Cartesian product, it must be an ordered pair. Let $z = (x, y)$.

1. Since $(x, y) \in A \times B$, it follows that $x \in A$ and $y \in B$.
    
2. Since $(x, y) \in C \times D$, it follows that $x \in C$ and $y \in D$.
    
3. Because $x \in A$ and $x \in C$, by definition of intersection, $x \in A \cap C$.
    
4. Because $y \in B$ and $y \in D$, by definition of intersection, $y \in B \cap D$.
    

By the definition of Cartesian product, since the first coordinate $x \in (A \cap C)$ and the second coordinate $y \in (B \cap D)$, the pair $(x, y)$ belongs to $(A \cap C) \times (B \cap D)$.

Therefore, $(A \times B) \cap (C \times D) \subseteq (A \cap C) \times (B \cap D)$.

**Part 2: $(\supseteq)$ Reverse Inclusion**

We simplify the Right Hand Side (RHS) to show it is contained in the Left Hand Side (LHS).

Let $w$ be an arbitrary element of $(A \cap C) \times (B \cap D)$.

By definition, $w$ is an ordered pair $(x, y)$ such that $x \in A \cap C$ and $y \in B \cap D$.

1. Since $x \in A \cap C$, we know $x \in A$ and $x \in C$.
    
2. Since $y \in B \cap D$, we know $y \in B$ and $y \in D$.
    
3. Consider the sets $A$ and $B$: Since $x \in A$ and $y \in B$, then $(x, y) \in A \times B$.
    
4. Consider the sets $C$ and $D$: Since $x \in C$ and $y \in D$, then $(x, y) \in C \times D$.
    

Since $(x, y)$ is an element of both $A \times B$ and $C \times D$, it must be in their intersection.

Therefore, $w \in (A \times B) \cap (C \times D)$.

Thus, $(A \cap C) \times (B \cap D) \subseteq (A \times B) \cap (C \times D)$.

**Conclusion:**

Since we have shown that each set is a subset of the other, we conclude that:

$$(A \times B) \cap (C \times D) = (A \cap C) \times (B \cap D)$$

### 1. The Scenario: Winter vs. Summer

Imagine you have two "Looks":

- **Look 1 (Winter):** $A = \{\text{Sweater}\}$, $B = \{\text{Jeans}\}$.
    - Product ($A \times B$) = $\{ (\text{Sweater}, \text{Jeans}) \}$

This is the **Union of Products**.

- **Logic:** "Take all the valid Winter outfits **OR** all the valid Summer outfits."

    1. (Sweater, Jeans)

### 1. The Visual Proof (The "L-Shape")

Think of $A \times B$ as a large rectangular sheet of paper.
