> [!note] Definition of the Trigonometric Functions
Let $t$ be any real number and let $P(x,y)$ be the terminal point on the unit circle determined by $t$.
>
We define
$\sin t=y$
$\cos t=x$
$\tan t= \frac{y}{x}$
$\csc t= \frac{1}{y}$ which is $\csc t=\frac{1}{\sin t}$
$\sec t=\frac{1}{x}$ which is $\frac{1}{\cos t}$
$\cot t= \frac{x}{y}$ which is $\frac{1}{\tan t}$


This is why the trigonometric function is a oscillating function because it is defined by the $P(x,y)$ on unit circle. Sometimes it is called the circular function.

==Domain of the Trigonometric Functions==

$\sin$ $\cos$: all real number

$\tan,\sec$: all real number other than $\frac{\pi}{2}+n\pi$ for any integer $n$

$\cot,\csc$: all real number other than $n\pi$ for any integer $n$

Why? $\cot=\frac{1}{\tan}$, thus $\tan =0$ when $t=n\pi$ because $y=0$. 
$\csc= \frac{1}{\sin}$, $\sin =0$ when $y=0$ when $t=n\pi$.

The range of the sine and cosine functions is the interval $[-1,1]$ . We can see this from the definition because the values of these functions are coordinates of points on the unit circle

==Fundamental Identities== ^39f00f

Reciprocal identities
$$
\begin{align}
\csc t&= \frac{1}{\sin t} \\
\sec t&= \frac{1}{\cos t} \\
\cot t&= \frac{1}{\tan t} \\
\tan t&= \frac{\sin t}{\cos t} \\
\cot t&= \frac{\cos t}{\sin t}
\end{align}
$$

Pythagorean Identities
$$
\begin{align}
\sin ^{2}t+\cos ^{2}
t&=1 \\ 
\tan ^{2}t+1&=\sec ^{2} t \\
1+\cot ^{2}&=\csc ^{2}t
\end{align}
$$

Proof:
By definition $\cos t=x$ and $\sin t=y$ where $P(x,y)$ is the coordinate lies on the unit circle. Thus, it follow that $x^{2}+y^{2}=1\implies \cos ^{2}t+\sin ^{2}t=1$


The second and third derived by dividing both side by $\cos ^{2}t$ or $\sin ^{2}t$. $Q.E.D$ 

As their name indicates, the fundamental identities play a central role in trigonometry because we can use them to relate any trigonometric function to any other.
