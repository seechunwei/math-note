## Local Extreme Values

> [!theorem]  The First Derivative Test
> Suppose that $c$ is a critical number of a continuous function $f$.
> 
> a) If $f'$ change from positive to negative at $c$, then $f$ has a local maximum at $c$.
> b) If $f'$ change from negative to positive at $c$, then $f$ has a local minimum at $c$.
> c) If $f'$ is positive(or negative) to the left and right of $c$, then $f$ has no local maximum or minimum at $c$.

## Concavity

> [!definition]
> If the graph of $f$ lies above all of its tangents on an interval $I$, then it is called **concave upward** on $I$ (slope increasing, smile face). If the graph of $f$ lies below all of its tangents on $I$, it is called **concave downward** on $I$. (slope decreasing, crying face)
> 

> [!theorem] Concavity Test
> 
> a) If $f''(x)> 0$ for all $x$ in $I$, then the graph of $f$ is concave upward on $I$.
> b) If $f''(x)<0$ for all $x$ in $I$, then the graph of $f$ is concave downward on $I$.

> [!definition]
> A point $P$ on a curve $y=f(x)$ is called an **inflection point** if $f$ is continuous there and the curve changes from concave upward to concave downward or vice versa at $P$.
> 
> Or $f''(x)=0$ 

> [!theorem]
> The Second Derivative Test
> Suppose $f''$ is continuous near $c$.
> 
> a) If $f'(c)=0$ and $f''(c)>0$, then $f$ has a local minimum at $c$.
> b) If $f'(c)=0$ and $f''(c)<0$, then $f$ has a local maximum at $c$.
