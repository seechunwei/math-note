==Theorem== 4.13
Let $x,y \in \mathbb{R}$. If $xy= 0$, then $x=0$ or $y=0$.

Assume that $xy=0$. We consider two cases, according to whether $x=0$ or $x\neq 0$.

Case1. $x=0$. 
Then we have the desired conclusion.

Case 2: $x\neq 0$
If $x\neq 0$.
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{1}{x}(xy)&=\left( \frac{1}{x} \right)(0) \\
y&=0
\end{align}
$$

In either case $x=0$ or $y=0$. Thus, the statement is true. Q.E.D

==Example 4.18==
Show that if $|x-1|< 1$ and $|x-1|< \frac{r}{4}$, where $r \in \mathbb{R}^{+}$, then $|x^{2}+x-2|< r$.

Proof:
Notice that $|x^{2}+2-2|=|(x-1)(x+2)|=|x-1||x+2|$
$$
\begin{align}
|x-1|< \frac{r}{4}
\end{align}
$$

By the triangle inequality
$$
\begin{align}
|x+2|&=|(x-1)+3| \\
&\leq |x-1|+|3| \\
&< 1+3 \\
&<4
\end{align}
$$

Thus,
$$
\begin{align}
|x^{2}+2-2|&\leq|x-1||x+2| \\
&< \frac{r}{4}(4) \\
&< r
\end{align}
$$
Q.E.D
