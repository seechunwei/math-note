
Addition and Subtraction Formulas
Formulas for Sine:
$$
\begin{align}
\sin(s+t)&=\sin s\cos t+\cos s\sin t \\
\sin(s-t)&=\sin s\cos t-\cos s\sin t
\end{align}
$$

Formulas for Cosine:
$$
\begin{align}
\cos(s+t)&=\cos s\cos t-\sin s\sin t \\
\cos(s-t)&=\cos s\cos t+\sin s\sin t
\end{align}
$$
Formulas for Tangent:
$$
\begin{align}
\tan(s+t)&= \frac{\tan s+\tan t}{1-\tan s\tan t} \\
\tan(s-t)&= \frac{\tan s-\tan t}{1+\tan s\tan t}
\end{align}
$$




Proof of Addition Formula for Cosine

$$
\cos(s+t)=\cos s\cos t-\sin s\sin t

Notice that $\cos(-s)=\cos s$ and $\sin(-s)=-\sin s$. Thus,
$Q_{0}(\cos s,-\sin s)$. Since the arc form by $P_{0}P_{1}$ and $Q_{0}Q_{1}$. It follow that $d(P_{0},P_{1})=d(Q_{0},Q_{1})$. Thus,

$$
\begin{align}
\sqrt{ (\cos(s+t)-1)^{2}+(\sin(s+t)-0)^{2} }&=\sqrt{ (\cos t-\cos s)^{2}+(\sin t+\sin s)^{2} } \\
\cos ^{2}(s+t)-2\cos(s+t)+1+\sin ^{2}(s+t)&=\cos ^{2}t
-2\cos s\cos t+\cos ^{2}s+\sin ^{2}t+2\sin t\sin s+\sin ^{2}s \\
\end{align}
$$

Notice that
$$
\cos ^{2}(s+t)+\sin ^{2}(s+t)=1
$$
and
$$
\cos ^{2}t+\sin ^{2}t=1
$$
$$
\cos ^{2}s+\sin ^{2}s=1
$$

Thus,
$$
\begin{align}
2-2s\cos(s+t)&=2-2\cos s\cos t+2\sin s\sin t \\
\cos(s+t)&=\cos s\cos t-\sin s\sin t
\end{align}
$$
Q.E.D

Proof of Subtraction Formula for Cosine
Replacing $t$ with $-t$ in the Addition Formula for Cosine, we obtain
$$
\begin{align}
\cos(s-t)&=\cos(s+(-t)) \\
&=\cos s\cos(-t)-\sin s\sin(-t) \\
&=\cos s\cos t+\sin s\sin t
\end{align}
$$
Q.E.D


Exercise 77 Addition Formula for Sine

Proof:

$$
\begin{align}
\sin(s+t)&=\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{2}-(s+t) \right) \\
&=\cos\left( \left( \frac{\pi}{2}-s \right)-t \right) \\
&=\cos \left( \frac{\pi}{2}-s \right)\cos(t)+\sin\left( \frac{\pi}{2}-s \right)\sin t \\
&=\sin s\cos t+\cos s\sin t
\end{align}
$$

Exercise 78 Addition Formula for Tangent

$$
\begin{align}
\tan(s+t)&= \frac{\sin(s+t)}{\cos(s+t)} \\
&=\frac{\sin s\cos t+\cos s\sin t}{\cos s\cos t-\sin s\sin t} \\
&= \frac{\frac{\sin s}{\cos s}+ \frac{\sin t}{\cos t}}{1- \frac{\sin s\sin t}{\cos s\cos t}} \\
&=\frac{\tan s+\tan t}{1-\tan s\tan t}
\end{align}
$$
Q.E.D

### Proving a Cofunction Identity

$$
\cos\left(  \frac{\pi}{2}-u \right)=\sin u
$$

By the Subtraction Formula for Cosine we have

$$
\begin{align}
\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{2}-u \right)&=\cos \frac{\pi}{2}\cos u+\sin \frac{\pi}{2}\sin u \\
&=0\cos u+1\sin u \\
&=\sin u
\end{align}
$$

### Expressions Involving Trigonometric Functions of a Sum


#### Simplifying an Expression Involving Inverse Trigonometric Functions

Write $\sin(\cos ^{-1}x+\tan ^{-1}y)$ as an algebraic expression in $x$ and $y$ where $-1\leq x\leq 1$ and $y$ is any real number.

Let $\theta=\cos ^{-1}x$ and $\theta_{1}=\tan ^{-1}y$. Since we know that $T_{1}(T_{2}^{-1}(x))$ can be always written as algebraic expression, it follow that

$\sin \theta=\sqrt{ 1-x^{2} }$  $\cos \theta_{1}= \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 1+y^{2} }}$ $\sin \theta_{1}=\frac{y}{\sqrt{ 1+y^{2} }}$
[[Trigonometric to algebraic]]

Notice that $\sin \theta_{1}$ can be negative, and the algebraic expression work perfectly.


We can get this from operate the trigonometric function and using cancellation rule, or we construct a triangle.

![[Pasted image 20260609135443.png]]

Thus, 

$$
\begin{align}
\sin(\cos ^{-1}x+\tan ^{-1}y)&=\sin(\theta+\theta_{1}) \\
&=\sin \theta \cos \theta_{1}+\cos \theta \sin \theta_{1} \\
&=\sqrt{ 1-x^{2} }\cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 1+y^{2} }}+x \frac{y}{\sqrt{ 1+y^{2} }} \\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 1+y^{2} }}(\sqrt{ 1-x^{2} }+xy)
\end{align}
$$
