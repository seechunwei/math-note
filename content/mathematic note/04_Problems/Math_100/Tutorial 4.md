c) If $m>n >0$ must $m^{2}-n^{2}$ be composite?

The statement can be formalize to
$$
\forall m,n \in \mathbb{Z},m>n >0\to m^{2}-n^{2} \text{ is composite}
$$
 Suppose m and n are arbitrary integer and $m>n >0$. Note that $m^{2}-n^{2}=(m-n)(m+n)$ .
 Since $m>n$ , thus $m- n>0$. Since $m>n >0$, it follow that $m^{2}-n^{2}>0$ and $m+n >0$ . Since both of the divisor and the product are positive it follow that $(m-n)\leq m^{2}-n^{2}$ and $(m+n)\leq m^{2}-n^{2}$  
Thus, $1\leq(m-n),(m+n)\leq m^{2}-n^{2}$

Case 1: If $m-n$>1 and $m+n$ >1, then $m^{2}-n^{2}$ is composite.

Case 2: If $m-n =1$, then $m+n =2k+1$ for some integer k. 
Thus, $m^{2}-n^{2}$ can be prime or composite.

Thus, the statement is false.
