We sketch the graph on the interval $\left(- \frac{\pi}{2} , \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ Notice that $-\frac{\pi}{2}$ and $\frac{\pi}{2}$ are vertical asymptotes of $\tan t$. 

$x\to \frac{\pi}{2}^{-}\implies \tan x\to \infty$

$x\to - \frac{\pi}{2}^{+}\implies \tan x\to-\infty$

### The graph of $\csc$ and $\sec$

![[Pasted image 20260428135310.png]]

The 0 become asymptote. The behavior of the graphs near the vertical asymptotes depends on the sign of the denominators.

![[Pasted image 20260604215444.png]]

Notice the secant graph, the asymptotes is when $\cos t=0$ which is when $t=\frac{\pi}{2}+n\pi$

Notice the cosecant graph, the asymptotes is when $\sin t=0$, which is when $t=n\pi$

From the graph we see that the range of the secant and cosecant functions is $(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty)$.

Even though the period is $2\pi$, but the period for asymptotes is every $\pi$. which is $n\pi$.
