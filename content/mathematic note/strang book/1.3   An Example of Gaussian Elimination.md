### The cost of elimination

How many separate arithmetical operations does elimination require, for n equations in n unknowns?

Since all the steps are known, we should be able to predict the number of operations.

We define operation into 2 ways
1) We divide by the pivot to find out what multiple (say) of the pivot equation is to be subtracted (The division step)

This operation include the step of eliminate the entry below the pivot in pivot column. (We can just put 0, instead of calculating)

2) We need to subtract the following entry in the row by the multiple of the entry above it (multiplication-subtraction)

For example,
$$\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{2} & \mathbf{4} & \mathbf{-2} \\
4 & 9 & -3 \\
-6 & -3 & 7 
\end{bmatrix}$$
There is $n^{2}$ entry in this matrix which is $3^{2}=9$


 2. Counting the Operations

The text defines an operation as either finding a multiplier or calculating a new entry.

 Processing Row 2:

1. **Find the multiple:** $4 \div 2 = 2$. (**1 operation**)
    
2. **Find new entries:** * Update second entry: $9 - (2 \times 4) = 1$. (**1 operation**)
    - Update third entry: $-3 - (2 \times -2) = 1$. (**1 operation**)
- **Total for Row 2:** $1 + 2 = \mathbf{3}$ **operations**. (Which is $n$)
    

 Processing Row 3:

1. **Find the multiple:** $-6 \div 2 = -3$. (**1 operation**)
    
2. **Find new entries:** * Update second entry: $-3 - (-3 \times 4) = 9$. (**1 operation**)
    - Update third entry: $7 - (-3 \times -2) = 1$. (**1 operation**)
- **Total for Row 3:** $1 + 2 = \mathbf{3}$ **operations**. (Which is $n$)

Thus, for the first round which is the original matrix we have $9-3=6$ operation which is $n^{2}-n$.

How it work?
There is 6 entry that need to under change, which is the entry except first row which is $9-3=6$

When it come to second round. The matrix will reduce to submatrix (ignore the first row and first column). It become a $2\times 2$ matrix. There exists $4$ entries in the matrix. Delete the first row, the entry under change is $4-2=2$. Thus, it is $(3-1)^{2}-(3-1)=4-2=2$.

|**Stage**|**Active Matrix Size (k)**|**Operations Needed (k2−k)**|
|---|---|---|
|1|$n$|$n^2 - n$|
|2|$n-1$|$(n-1)^2 - (n-1)$|
|3|$n-2$|$(n-2)^2 - (n-2)$|
|...|...|...|
|$n-1$|$2$|$2^2 - 2 = 2$|
|$n$|$1$|$1^2 - 1 = 0$ (No rows left below the last pivot)|

The formula $\sum_{k=1}^n (k^2 - k)$ is just a mathematically concise way of writing:

$$(n^2 - n) + ((n-1)^2 - (n-1)) + \dots + (2^2 - 2) + (1^2 - 1)$$

In mathematics, the order of summation doesn't matter. Whether you count from the first stage ($n$) down to the last ($1$), or from the last up to the first, the total is the same.

(We can just think in what entry being change during the elimination step)

For large matrices, the $n^3$ term dominates. This is why you will often hear that Gaussian elimination has a complexity of **roughly $\frac{1}{3}n^3$ operations**. This summation is the formal proof of where that "cubic" complexity comes from: you are essentially summing the areas of shrinking squares.

Back-substitution happens after you have already transformed your matrix into an **upper triangular** form ($U$). At this point, the "heavy lifting" of clearing out the bottom of the matrix is done, and you are just solving for the variables one by one, starting from the bottom.

Here is why the operation count follows the arithmetic progression $1 + 2 + \dots + n$.

### 1. The Last Unknown ($x_n$)

The very last equation in your triangular system looks like this:

$$u_{nn}x_n = d_n$$

To find $x_n$, you only need to perform **one division**: $x_n = d_n / u_{nn}$.

- **Total Operations:** 1
    

### 2. The Second to Last Unknown ($x_{n-1}$)

The second to last equation involves two variables, $x_{n-1}$ and $x_n$:

$$u_{n-1,n-1}x_{n-1} + u_{n-1,n}x_n = d_{n-1}$$

Since you already know $x_n$ from the previous step, you do two things:

1. **Multiply and Subtract:** Calculate $d_{n-1} - (u_{n-1,n} \cdot x_n)$. (**1 operation**)
    
2. **Divide:** Divide the result by the pivot $u_{n-1,n-1}$. (**1 operation**)
    

- **Total Operations:** 2
    

### 3. The General Pattern

As you move up the matrix, each new row has one more "already solved" variable than the row below it.

- **Row 3 from bottom:** Requires 2 multiplication-subtractions and 1 division = **3 operations**.
    
- **Row $k$ from bottom:** Requires $k-1$ multiplication-subtractions and 1 division = **$k$ operations**.
    

By the time you reach the **top row**, you are substituting in all $(n-1)$ previously found values and doing one final division.

- **Total Operations for the top row:** $n$
    

---

### 4. The Grand Total: $O(n^2)$

When you sum these up, you get the famous triangular number formula:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} k = 1 + 2 + 3 + \dots + n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$$
