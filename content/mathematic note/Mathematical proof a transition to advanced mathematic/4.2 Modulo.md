Result 4.9
Let $a,b,k$ and $n$ be integers where $n\geq 2$. If $a\equiv b \pmod n$, then $ka\equiv kb \pmod n$.

==Proof strategy==
$a\equiv b \pmod n$ means that $n\mid (a-b)$ which mean that $a-b=nx$ for some $x \in \mathbb{Z}$. Thus,
$ka-kb=knx$.

We need to prove $ka\equiv kb \pmod n$. Thus,
$n\mid (ka-kb)$, thus $ka-kb=ny$ for some $y \in\mathbb{Z}$.

Proof:
Suppose $a,b,n,k\in \mathbb{Z}$ and $n\geq 2$.
Suppose $a\equiv b \pmod n$. Then, $n\mid (a-b)$. Hence,  $a-b=nx$ for some $x \in \mathbb{Z}$. Therefore, s
$$
\begin{align}
ka-kb&=knx \\
&=n(kx)
\end{align}
$$

Since $kx$ is an integer, it follow that $n\mid(ka-kb)$. Thus, $ka\equiv kb \pmod n$. Q.E.D.

Result 4.10
Let $a,b,c,d,n \in \mathbb{Z}$ where $n\geq 2$. If $a\equiv b \pmod n$ and $c\equiv d \pmod n$, then $a+c\equiv b+d \pmod n$

Proof:
Suppose $a\equiv b \pmod n$ and $c\equiv d \pmod n$ . Thus, $a-b=nx$ and $c-d=ny$ for some $x,y \in \mathbb{Z}$. 

$$
\begin{align}
(a-b)+(c-d)&=nx+ny \\
(a+c)-(b+d)&=n(x+y)
\end{align}
$$
Since $x+y$ is an integer, $n\mid[(a+c)-(b+d)]$. Thus, $a+c\equiv b+d \pmod n$

**The Property of Sub-moduli:**

If $a \equiv b \pmod{n}$ and $m$ is a divisor of $n$, then $a \equiv b \pmod{m}$.

Since **2 is a divisor of 6**, we can conclude:

> If $x \equiv 1 \pmod{6}$, then $x \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$ is **always true.**

In this direction, the relationship is **many-to-one**. Any number that leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by 6 will _always_ be odd. The same applies to other residues:

- If $x \equiv 3 \pmod{6}$, then $x \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$ (because $3$ is odd).
    
- If $x \equiv 5 \pmod{6}$, then $x \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$ (because $5$ is odd).
