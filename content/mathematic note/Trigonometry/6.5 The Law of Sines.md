
If we map triangle to angle, it is not one-to-one function. (many to one) (This is the reason why we need at least one side of the triangle to determine the triangle)

if we map angle to trigonometric function value, it is many to one function.(Exp: $T(2\pi+\theta)=T(\theta)$).

But in the case of triangle in real life, angle to trigonometric function mapping is a one-to-one function. (That's why we can use this information to establish a relationship between the angle and trigonometric function value of a triangle)

But how about the triangle? What if we are given one side of triangle? Then we can infer about the triangle.

Let's consider some of the example
![[Pasted image 20260607111056.png]]

Case 1: One side and two angle (ASA or SAA)
Case 2: Two side and the angle opposite one of these (SSA)
Case 3: Two sides and the included angle (SAS)
Case 4: Three sides (SSS)

Case 1 and 2 are solved by using Law of Sines, Cases 3 and 4 require the Law of Cosines.

> [!info] The Law of Sines
In triangle $ABC$ we have
>
>$$
\frac{\sin A}{a}=\frac{\sin B}{b}= \frac{\sin C}{c}
$$

Proof: By the formula given in section 6.3, the area of triangle $ABC$ is

$$
\frac{1}{2}bc\sin \theta=\frac{1}{2}ac\sin \theta= \frac{1}{2}ab \sin \theta
$$

(It is combination of 2 out of 3 $^{3}C_{2}$) . Thus, multiplying by $\frac{2}{abc}$, it gives the Law of Sines.

Notice that for case 1, the triangle formed unique. But this is not the case for case 2 $(SSA)$ 

Consider angle $A$ and sides $a,b$ are given

![[Pasted image 20260607160453.png]]

a) The side $a$ is too short to form a triangle
b) It is a right triangle
c) The length of $a$ is greater than the perpendicular implies that $a$ can form 2 hypotenuse
d) The length of $a$ is long enough to only form one unique triangle


==Example== (one solution case)
Solve triangle $ABC$, where $\angle A=45^\circ$ , $a=7\sqrt{ 2 }$ and $b=7$

![[Pasted image 20260607160914.png]]

To solve the triangle. we can find $\angle B$

$$

^125458

\begin{align}
\frac{\sin 45 }{7\sqrt{ 2 }}&= \frac{\sin B}{7} \\
\sin B&= \frac{1}{2}
\end{align}
$$

Thus, $B=\sin ^{-1}\left(  \frac{1}{2} \right)$ . $B \in[0,180]$. (Quadrant I and II). Thus, $B=30^\circ$ or $B=150^\circ$.

Since $150^\circ+45^\circ>180^\circ$, it follow that $B=30^\circ$. Thus, this example is the example for case d, only one unique triangle is formed.
