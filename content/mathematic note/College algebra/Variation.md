> [!note] direct variation
>
We say $y$ varies directly with $x^{n}$ when $y=kx^{n}$ where $k$ is called the constant of variation. (One quantity is a constant multiply with another quantity). 
>
When $x^{n}$ increase by $d$ times, $y$ also increase/decrease by $d$ times. If we draw a graph $y$ over $x^{n}$, we will get a straight line. If we draw a graph $y$ over $x$ we will get a power function graph.

inverse variation

We say $y$ varies inversely with $nth$ power of $x$ denoted by $y=\frac{k}{x^{n}}$ where $k$ is constant variation. 

Or in other word, their product is a constant denoted by $yx^{n}=k$. Notice that the magnitude of $x^{n}$ become bigger when $y$ become smaller and the other way around.

The difference of direct variation and inverse direction is the change of magnitude between 2 variable. 

Or it is difference between arithmetic and geometric. One is a constant rate of change while the another is percent change (倍数增长)


joint variation
When a variable is dependent on the product or quotient of two or more variables, this is called joint variation.

For instance, if x varies directly with both y and z, we have $x=kyz$. If x varies directly with y and inversely with z, we have $x=\frac{ky}{z}$. Notice that we only use one constant in a joint variation equation.
