$(A\cup B)-(A\cap B)=(A-B)\cup(B-A)$

Proof:
Suppose $x \in(A\cup B)-(A\cap B)$. Thus, $x \in A\cup B$ and $x\not\in A \cap B$. Since $x \in A\cup B$ it follow that $x \in A$ or $x \in B$. WLOG, let $x \in A$
Since $x \not\in A\cap B$, it follow that $x \not\in B$. Thus , $x \in A-B$.. Thus, $(A\cup B)-(A\cap B)\subseteq(A-B)\cup(B-A)$

Conversely, suppose $x \in (A-B)\cup(B-A)$. Thus, $x \in A\cap B^{c}$ or $x \in B\cap A^{c}$. Thus, $x \in A\cup B$ and $x \not\in A\cap B$. Thus, $x \in(A\cup B)-(A\cap B)$. $(A-B)\cup(B-A)\subseteq(A\cup B)-(A\cap B)$

Proof:
$$
\begin{align}
(A\cup B)-(A\cap B)&=(A\cup B)\cap(A\cap B)^{c} \\
&=(A\cup B)\cap(A^{c}\cup B^{c}) \\
&=(A\cup B\cap A^{c})\cup(A\cup B\cap B^{c}) \\
&=(B\cup A^{c})\cup(A\cup B^{c}) \text{ (absorption law)} \\
&=(B-A)\cup(A-B)
\end{align}
$$


Result 4.21
Let A and B be sets. Then $A\cup B=A$ iff $B\subseteq A$.

Proof:
Forward direction
Suppose $x \in B$, thus $x \in A\cup B$. Since $A\cup B\subseteq A$. It follow that $x \in A$.

Backward direction.
Suppose $x \in A\cup B$. Thus, $x \in A$ or $x \in B$.

Case 1: $x \in A$
Then we are done

Case 2: $x \in B$
Since $B\subseteq A$, it follow that $x \in A$.

In either case, $x \in A$, thus $A\cup B\subseteq A$
Conversely, suppose $x \in A$. Thus $x \in A\cup B$.
Therefore, $A\subseteq A\cup B$

Since $A\cup B\subseteq A$ and $A\subseteq A\cup B$. It follow that $A\cup B=A$.
