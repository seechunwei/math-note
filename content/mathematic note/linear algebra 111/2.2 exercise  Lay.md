What is the purpose of finding the inverse of A in solving Ax=b
#### . The Algebraic Solution

If $A$ is an invertible $n \times n$ matrix, we can multiply both sides of the equation by $A^{-1}$ from the **left**:

$$A^{-1}(A\mathbf{x}) = A^{-1}\mathbf{b}$$

Because matrix multiplication is associative, we can group $A^{-1}$ and $A$ together:

$$(A^{-1}A)\mathbf{x} = A^{-1}\mathbf{b}$$

Since $A^{-1}A = I_n$ (the identity matrix):

$$I_n\mathbf{x} = A^{-1}\mathbf{b}$$

$$\mathbf{x} = A^{-1}\mathbf{b}$$

This provides a direct formula to find the solution vector $\mathbf{x}$ without having to perform row reduction every single time.

Instead of re-solving the entire system using Gaussian elimination for every new $\mathbf{b}$, you calculate $A^{-1}$ **once**. Then, for every new $\mathbf{b}$, you simply perform a quick matrix-vector multiplication ($A^{-1}\mathbf{b}$) to find your new $\mathbf{x}$.

==Remark==
The inverse of a lower triangular matrix is a lower triangular matrix.

We need to find a formula. Suppose $A$ is a lower triangular matrix and $B=A^{-1}$ . Then

$$
b_{ij}= -\frac{1}{a_{ii}}\sum_{k=j}^{i-1}a_{ik}b_{kj} 
$$  for $i>j$. Why?

We know that $AB=I_{n}$ . Thus $(AB)_{ij}=0$ when $i>j$ (more general odd diagonal). Thus,

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_{ik}b_{kj} = 0$$
Because $A$ is lower triangular, $a_{ik}=0$ when $k>i$
and $b_{kj}=0$ when $k<j$. Thus, $j<k<i$. Thus, $k$ start from $j$ and less than $i$


$$\sum_{k=j}^{i} a_{ik}b_{kj} = 0$$

We want to find a formula for a specific unknown entry of the inverse, $b_{ij}$. Let's pull the very last term ($k = i$) out of that summation:

$$\left( \sum_{k=j}^{i-1} a_{ik}b_{kj} \right) + a_{ii}b_{ij} = 0$$

Now, we just use basic algebra to isolate $b_{ij}$:

1. Move the summation to the right side:
    
    $$a_{ii}b_{ij} = -\sum_{k=j}^{i-1} a_{ik}b_{kj}$$
    
2. Divide both sides by $a_{ii}$ (which we assume is non-zero, otherwise $A$ wouldn't be invertible):
    
    $$b_{ij} = -\frac{1}{a_{ii}}\sum_{k=j}^{i-1}a_{ik}b_{kj}$$

### How about upper triangular matrix?

$(AB)_{ij}=0$ when $j>i$ (above diagonal part). Thus,


$$

\end{align}
$$

Since $A$ is upper triangular, $a_{ij}=0$ when $i>k$ and 
$b_{ij}=0$ when $k>j$. Thus $i<k<j$. Thus,

$$\sum_{k=i}^{j} a_{ik}b_{kj} = 0$$
