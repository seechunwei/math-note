
Notice that the $\sin$ and $\cos$ repeated its value every $2\pi$ which is the circumstance of a unit circle. 

It follow that the real number $t$ is the same as $t+2\pi$. Thus,
$$
\begin{align}
\sin(t+2n\pi)&=\sin t \\
\cos(t+2n\pi)&=\cos t
\end{align}
$$
for any integer $n$.

A function is periodic iff there exist a positive integer $p$ such that $f(x+p)=f(x)$ for any $x$.

Let's consider the graph of $\sin t$. Notice that
$\sin t=y$, and when $t=0$, $\sin t=0$,
$\sin t=1$, when $t= \frac{\pi}{2}$
$\sin t=0$ when $t= \pi$
$\sin t=-1$ when $t=\frac{3}{2}\pi$
$\sin t=0$ when $t=2\pi$ (one period)

The least such positive integer is called period.

> [!info] Definition
Period is the length of an interval where a function repeated its values regularly over successive intervals of the same length



> [!info] Periodic Properties of Sine and Cosine
The functions sine and cosine have period $2\pi$: 
>$$
\sin(t+2\pi)=\sin t \text{ and }\cos(t+2\pi)=\cos t
$$

So the sine and cosine functions repeat their values in any interval of length $2\pi$. Thus, to draw the graph, we just need to sketch an interval of length $2\pi$ and keep on repeating.

## 2. The Bounding Envelopes

Because the outputs of a standard $\sin x$ wave are strictly trapped between $-1$ and $1$, multiplying it by $\frac{1}{x}$ means that the entire graph of $f(x)$ is permanently trapped between two curves:

- The upper boundary: $y = \frac{1}{x}$
    
- The lower boundary: $y = -\frac{1}{x}$
    

These two hyperbola branches act as a guiding "envelope" or cage for the graph. The sine wave continues to oscillate at its normal frequency, but its peaks and troughs are forced to touch these shifting boundary lines.

## 3. Visualizing the Behavior Along the X-Axis

By tracking how the amplitude function $A(x) = \frac{1}{x}$ behaves, we can easily map out the entire graph logically:

- **As $x \to \infty$ (Moving Right):** The amplitude function $\frac{1}{x}$ approaches $0$. Because the amplitude is shrinking toward zero, the oscillations of the sine wave become heavily compressed vertically. The wave keeps oscillating back and forth across the x-axis, but the ripples get smaller and smaller, creating a **damped wave** effect that dies out at infinity.
    
- **As $x \to -\infty$ (Moving Left):** The same damping effect happens on the negative side. However, because $f(x)$ is an **even function** ($f(-x) = f(x)$), the graph is perfectly symmetrical across the y-axis. The decay looks identical on both sides.
    
- **As $x \to 0$ (The Center Peak):** As $x$ gets closer to $0$, the envelope $\frac{1}{x}$ shoots up toward infinity. However, the sine wave is also approaching $0$ at the exact same time. The structural logic of calculus shows that these two forces perfectly balance out at the center:
    
    $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1$$
    
    So, instead of exploding to infinity, the wave smoothly reaches a singular, maximum peak at the coordinate $(0, 1)$.
