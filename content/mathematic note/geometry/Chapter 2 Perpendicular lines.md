
### Right, acute and obtuse angles.

How to define right angle using straight angle and full angle?

>An angle of $90^\circ$ (congruent to one half of the straight angle or to one quarter of the full angle)

Acute angle

>An angle that smaller than the right angle

Obtuse angle

>An angle that bigger than the right angle and smaller than straight angle

Reflex angle

>more than $180^\circ$ and less than $360^\circ$

### Supplementary angles

Suppose there exist 2 angles with same vertex and have common side and the remaining side are continuation of each other.

Since the sum of such angles is a straight angle, the sum of two supplementary angles is $180^\circ$

Notice that each angle have 2 supplementary angle by prolonging both sides of the angle. Notice that 2 supplementary angle are congruent to each other because both are $180^\circ-C$ where $C$ is a constant angle.

![[Pasted image 20260329231424.png]]


Thus, if one of the four angles formed by two intersecting lines (AC and BD, Figure 23) is right, then the other three angles must be right as well. Why?

Supplementary angle $180^\circ-90^\circ=90^\circ$ . Thus, the remaining angle is full angle $360^\circ-270^\circ=90^\circ$.

### A perpendicular and a slant


Suppose there exists 2 supplementary angle $\angle AOD$ and $\angle DOB$, their common side $OD$ is a slant to the line $AB$ iff $\angle AOD \not\cong \angle DOB$ and conversely their common side $OD$ is a perpendicular to the line $AB$ iff $\angle AOD \cong \angle DOB$.

The common vertex is called foot of slant/ perpendicular.

### Vertical angles.

Two angles are called vertical if the sides of one of them form continuations of the sides of the other

Two vertical angles are congruent to each other (for e-ample, $\angle AOD\cong\angle COD$) since each of them is supplementary to the same angle

### Angles that have a common vertex. 

If two angles (AOB and BOC, Figure 24) have a common vertex (0) and a common side (OB) and add up to 2d (i.e.180°),then their two other sides (AO and OC) form continuations of each other (i.e. such angles are supplementary).
