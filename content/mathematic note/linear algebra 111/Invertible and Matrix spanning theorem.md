
#### The matrix spanning theorem for square matrix and not square matrix

For matrix to become invertible, first it must be a square matrix and also it must be one to one for any b such that $Ax=b$

Thus, square matrix that satisfy the condition of every row have pivot position also satisfy the condition of every column.

Thus, it have no free variable, thus it only have one solution for any $b$. 
For **square matrices**, they are actually **perfectly equivalent**.

What if it is not square matrix?
Then it might have at least one solution for any $b$. For example

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\
0 &  1& 3 & 6
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Notice that $x_{2}$ is free. Thus, it have infinitely many solution for any $b$. It is still spam the whole $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ space.
