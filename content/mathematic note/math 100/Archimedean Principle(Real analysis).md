For any 2 real number $a$ and $b$ with $a> 0$ , there exists a natural number $n$ such that $na>b$.

$$
\begin{align}
na&>b \\
n&> \frac{b}{a}
\end{align}
$$
Since $a>0$, thus we can divide the inequality by $a$. Notice that $\frac{b}{a}\in \mathbb{R}$.

Thus, we need to show that for any real number $x$, there exists a natural number $n$ such that $n>x$. (The simplified version)

SFC (Suppose for contradiction), there exists a real number $x$ such that $x\geq n$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$. 

Completeness Axiom
Thus $\mathbb{N}$ is bounded above $\implies$ $SUP(\mathbb{N})=s$. 
(s is supremum - the least upper bound of natural number)

Consider $s-1 \in \mathbb{N}$, since $s-1$ is not supremum, thus $\exists m \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $m>s-1$. Thus, $m+1>s$ which is a contradiction.
