solving the equation $Ax=b$ amounts to finding all vectors $x$ in $\mathbb{R}^{4}$ that are transformed into the vector $b$ in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$. It is like finding the preimage.

In this case the domain is $\mathbb{R}^{4}$ and the codomain is $\mathbb{R}^{2}$. Thus, it is a function/transformation means it must satisfy the 2 condition
1)assignment
2)unique

==Definition==
A transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to \mathbb{R}^{m}$ is a rule that assign each vector $x$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ a vector $T(x)$ in $\mathbb{R}^{m}$.

Thus the notation $T:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to \mathbb{R}^{2}$ indicate that the domain is $\mathbb{R}^{4}$ and codomain is $\mathbb{R}^{2}$. ($x \in Dom,b \in Cod$ and in fact $b=$range)

For $x$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$, the vector $T(x)$ in $\mathbb{R}^{m}$ is called the image of $x$ (under the action of $T$). The set of all images $T(x)$ is called the range of $T$.

### Matrix Transformations

Finding solution for $T(x)=b$ is like solving for $Ax=b$.

### Linear Transformation

A transformation $T$ is linear if

i) $T(u+v)=T(u)+T(v)$ for all $u,v$
in the domain of $T$.

ii) $T(cu)=cT(u)$ for all scalars $c$ and all $u$ in the domain of $T$.

Every matrix transformation is a linear transformation. Because matrix also hold these 2 properties.

Linear transformations preserve the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication. If we add 2 vector first in the domain and applying $T$ is the same as we applying $T$ to 2 vector and add together.

==Definition==
If $T$ is a linear transformation, then
$$
T(0)=0
$$
and
$$
T(cu+dv)=cT(u)+dT(v) \text{ (4)}
$$
for all vectors $u,v$ in the domain of $T$ and all scalars $c,d$.

Observe that if a transformation satisfies(4) for all $u,v$ and $c,d$ ,it must be linear.

The transformation $T(x)$ is defined by its action on the basis vectors:

$$T(x) = T(x_1e_1) + T(x_2e_2)$$

$$= x_1 T(e_1) + x_2 T(e_2)$$

$$= x_1 \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix} + x_2 \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$= \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix}$$

### Applying the Transformation

**Case 1: Let $v_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}$**

$$T(v_1) = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$= \begin{bmatrix} 13 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Case 2: Let $v_2 = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix}$**

$$T(v_2) = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$= \begin{bmatrix} 2x_1 - x_2 \\ 5x_1 + 6x_2 \end{bmatrix}$$
