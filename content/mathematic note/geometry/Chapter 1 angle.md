### Preliminary concepts

figure formed by two rays drawn from the same point is called an angle

The ray connect to the same point is called its sides

common endpoint is called the vertex of the angle.

$$
\angle AOB 
$$
The sides of angle divide the whole plane in to 2 regions. One is called interior region and another one is called exterior region. 

Commonly interior region is the region that contained a segment of any 2 point on the sides of angle.

Rays drawn from the vertex of an angle and lying in its interior(OD, OE, Figure 9) form new angles (AOD, DOE, EOB) which are considered to be parts of the angle (AOB).

### Congruent and non-congruent angles.

Suppose 2 angle $\angle AOB$ and $\angle CO'D$. We say $\angle AOB\cong\angle CO'D$ iff there exists an isometry that map the vertex $O$ to $O'$ and sides $OA$ to $O'C$ and $OB$ to $O'D$. (align perfectly)

and the interior regions of both angles lie on the same side of the line.

Notice that 90 and 270 in a circle is not congruent even though both angle sides coincide!!! (check the region/measure)

### Sum of angles
Suppose 2 angle $\angle AOB$ and $\angle CO'D$. We add these 2 angles by attach them together.

We first construct an angle $\angle MNP$ that congruent to $\angle AOB$. Then we attach it to the angle $\angle PNQ$ that congruent to $\angle CO'D$.

The sum of angle is the region that comprised by the interior regions of the summands.

Addition of angles obeys the commutativity and associativity laws just the same way addition of segments does.

Because $\angle AOB\cong \angle BOA$, if we add the angle together in opposite way the region is still the same, the only thing change is the location of sides which is still congruent.

They are a group.

Why we need to use the concept of congruent when we define addition and other operation?

Suppose we want to add two angle together and they are on different plane, we cannot simply attach them together. Thus, we use congruence to define the intrinsic value of angle which is independent from location.

### Extension of the concept of angle.

Straight angle - the both sides of angle happen to be the continuation of each other and form a straight line.

Full angle - we add 2 or more angle together and the last side and first side happen to be coincide.

### Central angle

The angle (AOB, Figure 16) formed by two radii of a circle is called a central angle.

Central angle is said to correspond to arc contained by 2 sides of the angle
#### Properties between corresponding Arcs and central angle

In one circle, or two congruent circles

Central angles are congruent iff the corresponding arcs are congruent.

Why?
arcs congruent
1) circle congruent
2) endpoint coincide

Notice the 2 condition in the statement congruent circle satisfy the 1 condition

The second condition central angles are congruent implies that both sides of angle coincide implies that the endpoints of the arc contained by 2 sides are coincide implies that arcs are congruent.

### Circular and angular degrees.

Suppose we divide a circle into 360 part (angle) that congruent to each other and connected to the center by radii.

By the properties of corresponding angle and arc, the arc is also divide into 360 part congruent to each other.

1//360 part of the circle is called angular degree and 1/360 part of arc is called circular degree.

Why 360 not other number?
Because 360 can be divided by many whole number and it is a legacy from base 60 system from **Sumerians** and **Babylonians** (around 3000–2000 BCE)

The degrees (both circular and angular) are further subdivided into 60 congruent parts called minutes, and the minutes are further subdivided into 60 congruent parts called seconds denoted by
$$
20^\circ 13' 23''
$$
