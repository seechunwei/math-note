Suppose a $m\times n$ matrix $A$, the theorem state that 

$$
rank(A)+\text{nullity}(A)=n
$$
- $\text{rank}(A)$ is the dimension of the **column space** (number of pivots).
    
- $\text{nullity}(A)$ is the dimension of the **nullspace** (number of free variables).

- $n$ is the total number of columns.

The rank determine the column space while the number of free variable determine the nullspace which is the solution set of $Ax=0$

Why for a square matrix a free variable means it cannot span the whole standard space but it not the case of wide matrix? 

It is because for a square matrix $n$ is consist of n pivot and 0 nullity while a wide matrix have $n$ pivot and $n-m$ nullity. Notice that the dimension of column space is determine by the rank which is the number of pivot.
