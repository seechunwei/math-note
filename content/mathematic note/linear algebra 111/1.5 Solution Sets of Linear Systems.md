### Homogeneous Linear Systems

A system of linear equations is said to be homogeneous if it can be written in the form $Ax=0$, where $A$ is an $m\times n$ matrix and 0 is the zero vector in $\mathbb{R}^{m}$

Why is not $Ux=0$, since entry of the vector is zero , it don't change if we perform any row operation.

Such a system $Ax=0$ always has at least one solution, namely $x=0$ (the zero vector in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$) (trivial solution)

the important question is whether there exists a non trivial solution?

> [!info]
> The homogeneous equation $Ax=0$ has a nontrivial solution if and only if the equation has at least one free variable.


### Solution in vector

==Example 1==
Determine if the following homogeneous system has a nontrivial solution
Suppose a matrix
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & -2  & 0\\
0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The solution set is 
$$
\begin{cases}
x_{1}=2x_{3} \\
x_{2}=0 \\
x_{3}\in \mathbb{R}
\end{cases}
$$
In vector form, the general solution is

$$
x=\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3} 
\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}
2x_{3} \\
0 \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=x_{3}
\begin{bmatrix}
2 \\
0 \\
1
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Here $x_{3}$ is factored out of the expression for the general solution vector. Notice that the solution set form $Span\{ v \}$ where $v$ is the vector. 

Notice that the vector $\bigl( \begin{smallmatrix} 2\\0\\1\end{smallmatrix} \bigr)$ is a point at space $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ and the origin which is the zero vector form a line.

==Example 2==
$$
10x_{1}-3x_{2}-2x_{3}=0
$$

can be express as 
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
10 & -3 & -2 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$x_{1}$ is basis variable while $x_{2},x_{3}$  are free variable. THus

$$
x=\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
3x_{2}+2x_{3} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
3x_{2} \\
x_{2} \\
0
\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}
2x_{3} \\
0 \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
=x_{2}\begin{bmatrix}
3 \\
1 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}+x_{3}\begin{bmatrix}
2 \\
0 \\
1
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Thus, the solution can be express as $Span\{ u,v \}$. Since neither u nor v is a scalar multiple of the other, the solution set is a plane through the origin

In conclusion, homogeneous equation $Ax=0$ can always be expressed as $Span\{ v_{1},\dots v_{n} \}$.


Why the solution of $Ax=b$ where $b$ is non-zero vector cannot be expressed as $Span\{ v \}$ given it is consistent and the $v$ is the solution set in vector?

It cannot be expressed as span because it cannot pass though the origin.

### Solution of Nonhomogeneous Systems

What is the solution of $Ax=b$ if a system has many solution when $Ax=0$? What is the relationship between them?

Consider the reduced echelon form of augmented matrix for $Ax=0$ below

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & -\frac{4}{3} & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
$$
This solution have infinity many solution because there exist a free variable $x_{3}$. Thus, the general solution set in parametric vector form denoted by

$$x=
\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
\frac{4}{3}x_{3} \\
0 \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=x_{3}\begin{bmatrix}
\frac{4}{3} \\
0 \\
1
\end{bmatrix}=x_{3}v
$$

where
$$
v=\begin{bmatrix}
\frac{4}{3} \\
0 \\
1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

What if $Ax=b$ For example, $b=(7,-1,-4)$

Thus, the reduced echelon form is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & -\frac{4}{3} & -1 \\
0 & 1 & 0 & 2 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The general solution set is 

$$
x=\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
-1+\frac{4}{3}x_{3} \\
2 \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
-1 \\
2 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}+x_{3} \begin{bmatrix}
\frac{4}{3} \\
0 \\
1
\end{bmatrix}
$$


Thus notice that the general solution for x can be express as

$$
x=p+tv
$$
where $tv=x_{3}\bigl( \begin{smallmatrix} \frac{4}{3} \\ 0 \\1\end{smallmatrix} \bigr)$ and 
$$
p=\begin{bmatrix}
-1 \\
2 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Notice that $tv$ is the solution for $Ax=0$. and the $p$ is the particular solution for $Ax=b$ when $t=0$.

#### What is their relationship geometrically?

$p$ is a translation to $v$ by adding $p$ to v. Thus, each point of line $v$ added by $p$. Thus, the line $p+v$ is parallel to the line $v$.

We call the equation $x=p+tv$ as the line though p parallel to v.

==Theorem 6==
Suppose the equation $Ax=b$ is consistent for some given $b$, (why? because $0\neq b$, thus the zero row must have 0 constant), and let $p$ be a solution. Then the solution set of $Ax=b$ is the set of all vectors of the form $x=p+v$ where $v$ is any solution of homogeneous equation $Ax=0$.
