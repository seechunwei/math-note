
==Definition==
A vector space is a nonempty set $V$ of objects, called vectors, on which are defined two operations, called addiction and multiplication by scalars (real number or complex number), subject to the ten axioms listed below for all vectors $u,v,w \in V$ and for all $c,d$ scalar.

1) The sum of $u$ and $v$ denoted by $u+v$ is in $V$. (addiction closure)
2) $u+v=v+u$
3) $(u+v)+w=u+(v+w)$
4) There is a zero vector $0$ such that $u+0=u$
5) For each $u$ in $V$, there is a vector $-u$ in $V$ such that $u+(-u)=0$

Notice that the first 5 vector is the properties of group.

6) The scalar multiple of $u$ by $c$, denoted by $cu$, is in $V$.
7) $c(u+v)=cu+cv$ (Distributive over vectors addition)
8) $(c+d)u=cu+du$ (Distributive over scalar addition)
9) $c(du)=(cd)u$ 
10) $1u=u$

==Theorem==
For any $u$ in a vector space $V$ over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ and scalar c,
a) Zero vector 0 of $u$ is unique (additive identity)
b) $(-u)$(additive inverse) of $u$ is unique for each $u$ in $V$
c) $0u=0$
d) $c{0}=0$
e) $-u=(-1)u$

### Alternative theorem on subspaces

> [!info] Theorem
A subset $W$ of a vector space $V$ over $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$) is a subspace of $V$ iff $W$ is a nonempty set and $\alpha u+\beta v$ is in $W$ for any $u,v$ in $W$ and (real or complex) scalars $\alpha$ and $\beta$. 
(This is to prove the 3 property of a subset of vector space is satisfy the 10 Axiom that being a vector space)

Proof:
Suppose that $W \subset V$ is a subspace over $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$). Then by definition, $W$ itself is a vector space. 

By Axiom 4, $W$ contains the zero vector 0. So $W$ is nonempty.

For $u$ and $v$ in $W$ and scalars $\alpha,\beta$ in $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$), by Axiom 6, $\alpha u$ and $\beta v$ is in $W$. Then, by Axiom 1, $\alpha u+\beta v$ id in $W$.

Conversely, suppose $W$ is a nonempty set in $V$ and $\alpha u+\beta v$ is in $W$ for any $u,v$ in $W$ and (real or complex) scalars $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

We need to show that $W$ is a vector space over $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$). Since $W$ is non empty, it follow that there exist an arbitrary element $w \in W$.

Let $\alpha=\beta=1$, thus $1w+1w \in W$.  Thus, $1w+1w \in V$. Thus, by Axiom 10, $1w+1w=w+w$. Thus, $w+w \in W$. (Axiom 1 satisfied)

For any w in W, they are also in the vector space V. So, by Axiom 1 for V, Axiom 2 and Axiom 3 are satisfied for W.

Now we need to prove for axiom 4. 
Since $W$ is nonempty, we can choose some vector $\mathbf{w} \in W$.

By setting the scalars $\alpha = 0$ and $\beta = 0$, the given condition guarantees that:

$$0\mathbf{w} + 0\mathbf{w} \in W$$

Since we know that $0\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{0}$ for any vector in $V$, this simplifies to:

$$\mathbf{0} + \mathbf{0} = \mathbf{0} \in W$$

Therefore, the zero vector $\mathbf{0}$ is in $W$.. (Axiom 4 satisfied)

For $w$ in $W$, there exists $-w\in V$ such that $w+(-w)=0$. Notice that $(-w)=-1(w)$. Thus,

$$
-w=-w+0=(-1)w+0w
$$
Thus, $-w \in W$. (Axiom 5 satisfied)


For any $w \in W$, we have

$$
aw=aw+0w
$$

Thus, $aw \in W$ for any $w \in W$. (Axiom 6 satisfy)

Since $u \in V$ , Axiom 7,8,9 and 10 is satisfied.

### A Subspace Spanned by a Set

==Theorem 1==
If $v_{1},\dots,v_{p}$ are in a vector space $V$, then $Span\{ v_{1},\dots,v_{p} \}$ is a subspace of $V$.

[[2.8 Subspace#^5776a2]]

==Theorem 1*== (Converse)
Suppose $W$ is a finite dimensional subspace of $V$, then $W$ can be written as Span$\{ v_{1},\dots,v_{p} \}$ for some finite set of vectors.

Proof:
Since $W$ is a finite dimensional subspace, there exist a finite basis such that $B=\{ v_{1},\dots,v_{p} \}$ for $W$ where $v_{1},\dots,v_{p}\in W$ 

By definition of basis, it follow that
$$
W=Span\{ v_{1},\dots,v_{p} \}
$$

Since $W$ is a subspace of $V$, all elements within $W$ (including our basis vectors) are automatically elements of $V$. (we need this statement because the theorem  1 state that $v_{1},\dots v_{p} \in V$). $\blacksquare$
